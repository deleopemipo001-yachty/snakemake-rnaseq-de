# Comparison with the original FPKM/limma preprint

Reproduction. The preprint's FPKM/limma-trend analysis was successfully replicated within this workflow. The analysis retained the same 12 samples (RC1–RC6, GSM4224323–GSM4224334) and identified 12,407 genes and 927 differentially expressed genes (DEGs), comprising 440 upregulated and 487 downregulated genes. The gene-level results were virtually identical to the original analysis, with a maximum difference of approximately 10⁻¹⁴.

Agreement with DESeq2. Among the 899 preprint DEGs that were testable by both methods, DESeq2 confirmed 896 (99.7%), with all genes showing consistent directions of expression change. Fold-change estimates also showed strong agreement, with Pearson and Spearman correlations of 0.95 and 0.97, respectively. Furthermore, 20 of the 22 pathways identified in the preprint were also significant in the DESeq2 analysis. These included major cell proliferation pathways such as cell cycle, DNA replication, p53, E2F, G2M, and MYC, alongside pathways associated with the loss of normal colonic functions.

Why DESeq2 identified more DEGs. The DESeq2 analysis detected 3,906 DEGs, largely because it evaluated approximately 7,400 additional low-expression genes excluded by the preprint's FPKM filtering criteria. Its use of raw count data and a paired experimental design also provided greater statistical power across the six sample pairs, identifying 2,137 DEGs compared with 899 on the shared genes. Additionally, pathways that were close to significance in the preprint, including TNFα/NF-κB signalling and epithelial–mesenchymal transition (EMT), became significant. The larger fold-change estimates from DESeq2 are partly attributable to the compression introduced by adding 1 before log2 transformation in the preprint's log2(FPKM+1) approach.

Differences and their explanations. A total of 525 preprint genes could not be directly compared. Of these, 122 were absent from the newer Ensembl 116 annotation, while 403 did not pass the raw-count filtering criteria. Most were short genes for which length normalisation allowed a relatively small number of reads to produce FPKM values of at least 1. Two smaller pathways reported in the preprint, nitrogen metabolism and renin–angiotensin signalling, were not reproduced. In addition, six pathways from the original preprint's KEGG analysis were unavailable in the 2011 legacy KEGG collection used in this workflow, preventing direct comparison.

Conclusion. Overall, the findings support the main biological conclusions of the preprint. The count-based DESeq2 pipeline independently reinforces the original findings, provides greater statistical power to detect differential expression, and addresses inaccuracies in the original sample description.
